For parents and teachers

Children learn music by listening, repeating, moving, experimenting, and creating. You do not need any music training to help. Curiosity is enough.

How adults can help

The activities are built so a child cannot get anything wrong. Your part is to keep it that way.

  • Sit nearby and listen. Your attention tells a child their sounds matter.
  • Ask open questions: "What did you notice?" "What happens if you change it?"
  • Describe instead of judging: "That one was higher" rather than "That was right."
  • Let them repeat. Repetition is how young children learn patterns.
  • Follow their lead. There is no order to finish and nothing to complete.
  • Stop while it is still fun. Ten happy minutes is plenty.

Before they start

  • Set the device volume to a comfortable level, especially with headphones.
  • Read and sign off on the terms, privacy and safety page. It takes about two minutes and is asked once per device.
  • Use the Reduce motion button if moving pictures bother your child.
  • Stay within reach of younger children while they play.

Music ideas for home

No instrument needed. Each idea matches an activity on this site.

Kitchen drum circle

Turn over a pot and a plastic tub. Tap a steady beat together, then take turns making up rhythms on top.

Goes with Feel the Beat

High and low voices

Make your voice slide up like a rocket and down like a slide. Reach up high and crouch down low as you go.

Goes with Explore Pitch

Turtle and rabbit walk

Put on any song. Walk slowly like a turtle, then quickly like a rabbit. Talk about how the speed feels.

Goes with Fast or Slow

Whisper and giant

Sing a favorite song in a whisper, then in a big giant voice. Which one fits a lullaby?

Goes with Loud or Soft

Sound hunt

Walk through your home and tap things gently with a wooden spoon. Sort the sounds: ringing, thumping, rattling.

Goes with Instrument World

Copy my clap

Clap a short pattern and let your child echo it. Then swap: they lead and you copy. Any pattern is a good one.

Goes with Remember the Pattern

Hum a made-up tune

Hum three or four notes about what you are doing. "We are wash-ing hands." Ask your child to add the next line.

Goes with Make a Melody

Draw the music

Play a piece from the list below. Give your child paper and crayons and ask, "What does this music look like?"

Goes with Music Drawing
